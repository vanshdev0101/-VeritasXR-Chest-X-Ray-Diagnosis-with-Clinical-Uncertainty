## 2 — The clinical problem

**Headline:** A confident wrong answer is worse than no answer

- Pneumonia is a leading cause of death in children and the elderly; the chest radiograph is the first-line investigation because it is fast, cheap and available
- Reading one reliably needs an experienced radiologist, and in most regions demand far exceeds supply
- Opacities are subtle, overlap with oedema and atelectasis, and shift with patient positioning
- Most deployed models return a label and a probability — and nothing that says *this is a case I do not understand*

**Notes:** This frames uncertainty as the research question, not as a feature.

## 4/5 — The gap and the hypothesis

**Three patterns run through those eleven papers:**

- **Accuracy looks solved, on easy data.** 95–99% is routine — but almost always on one small, single-source, often paediatric dataset. On adult multi-source RSNA data the same task is far harder (AP 51.5).
- **Pretraining, not architecture, moves the numbers.** Fine-tuning beats a frozen backbone by 5.5 points; self-supervised pretraining reaches radiologist level with no labels at all.
- **Uncertainty is unsettled.** A 2025 benchmark of 13 methods finds no single winner, and most studies report no uncertainty at all.

**The hypothesis:** can a compact dual-pathway CNN earn useful accuracy *and* a free uncertainty signal in one forward pass — and survive the replication these papers rarely report?

**The answer, over three seeds:** it splits. The single-pass uncertainty works (AUROC 0.7747, no ensemble). The accuracy does not follow.

## 6 — Research question

**Headline:** Does a purpose-built architecture beat transfer learning on 18,677 images?

Three questions, each answered with a measurement rather than an assertion:

1. Does the custom architecture outperform ImageNet-pretrained CNNs under one shared protocol?
2. Does each of its components earn its parameters?
3. Does pathway disagreement work as an uncertainty signal?

**Notes:** Panels respect a question you can fail. State all three now so the results section has a spine.

## 7 — Dataset and protocol

**Headline:** RSNA Pneumonia Detection Challenge — 26,684 radiographs

| Class | Images | Share |
|---|---|---|
| No Lung Opacity / Not Normal | 11,821 | 44.3% |
| Normal | 8,851 | 33.2% |
| Lung Opacity (positive) | 6,012 | 22.5% |

- Split **by patient**: 18,677 train / 4,001 validation / 4,006 test
- Stratified on the **three-class** label, so the hard middle class — abnormal but not pneumonia, 44% of the data — stays proportional
- `tests/test_no_leakage.py` asserts no patient crosses splits, and **fails when a leak is injected** — the test is verified, not assumed
- Every model reads one config: same loss, optimiser, LR, schedule, augmentation, early stopping. **Three seeds each.**

**Notes:** The shared protocol is what makes this a comparison rather than five separate experiments.

## 9 — Component 1: the stem

**Headline:** A deliberately wide first layer

- `Conv 11×11, stride 2` → BatchNorm → ReLU → `Conv 3×3` → BatchNorm → ReLU → `MaxPool 3×3, stride 2`
- Output: **64 × 56 × 56**, from 44,864 parameters (1.1% of the model)

**Why 11×11:** radiographic signal is diffuse texture, not sharp edges. A 3×3
first layer is tuned for natural-image edges; a wide stem matches what a
radiograph actually carries.

---

## 10 — Component 2: the bottleneck block

**Image:** `diagrams/2-bottleneck-block-{light|dark}.png`

**Headline:** 1×1 reduce → k×k → 1×1 expand, with a projection shortcut

- Three stages per pathway, **stride 2 on every stage**, so 56×56 becomes 7×7
- The k×k convolution runs at half width — that is the "bottleneck"
- The shortcut is a 1×1 stride-2 conv + BN, because stride and width both change

**The design lesson:** the first version of this model stacked three full-width
k×k convolutions. At k=7 and 256 channels, that one choice held **59% of a
17M-parameter model** and ran 4–5× slower than baselines with more parameters.
Bottlenecking took the model from 17,020,325 to 4,215,352 parameters.

---

## 11 — Component 3: the two pathways

**Headline:** Identical depth, identical widths, one difference — kernel size

| Pathway | Kernel | Parameters | Share | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local | 3×3 | 863,128 | 20.5% | 384 × 7 × 7 |
| **Global** | **7×7** | **3,156,888** | **74.9%** | 384 × 7 × 7 |

- Widths (128, 256, 384) and three stages are the same in both — the comparison is clean because *only* the receptive field differs
- Motivation: a radiologist reads local opacity texture and whole-lung structure together

**Measured:** removing the 7×7 global pathway costs accuracy on **all three
seeds** — the only component in the model that survives replication.

---

## 12 — Component 4: squeeze-and-excitation

**Image:** `diagrams/3-squeeze-excitation-{light|dark}.png`

**Headline:** Per-image channel attention at the end of each pathway

- **Squeeze:** global average pool, 384 × 7 × 7 → 384 numbers
- **Excite:** Linear 384→24, ReLU, Linear 24→384, sigmoid (reduction 16)
- **Scale:** multiply each channel of the original map by its learned gain

**Measured:** removing SE helps on seed 1 and hurts on seeds 0 and 2. The sign
flips. It is in the model and it is not doing measurable work.

---

## 13 — Component 5: the merge gate

**Image:** `diagrams/4-merge-gate-{light|dark}.png`

**Headline:** A blend weight predicted from the image, not a constant

- Both pathways pool to 384, concatenate to 768
- `Linear 768→64` → ReLU → `Linear 64→2` → softmax → two weights that sum to 1
- `merged = w_local · local + w_global · global`, then the classifier
- 49,346 parameters (1.2%)

**Three alternatives were trained and measured:** a fixed 50/50 average, one
global learned scalar, and concatenation. The global scalar — the original
design — converged to **0.510 / 0.490**, a plain average with extra steps. None
of the four separates reliably across seeds.

---

## 14 — Component 6: classifier and per-path heads

**Headline:** One shared decision, two independent ones

- **Classifier:** `384 → 256` (BN, ReLU, Dropout 0.3) `→ 2`. 99,586 parameters.
- **Per-path heads:** one `Linear(384, 2)` per pathway. 1,540 parameters total.

**Why the per-path heads exist:** each pathway must be independently predictive,
which pushes the two to differentiate instead of collapsing into duplicates.
They are also what makes disagreement measurable at all — the previous version
merged before the head, which makes pathway disagreement unobservable by
construction.

---

## 15 — Component 7: the uncertainty output

**Headline:** Two candidate signals, one forward pass, one survivor

| Signal | What it is | AUROC against the model's own errors |
|---|---|---|
| Pathway disagreement | Jensen-Shannon divergence between the two path heads | **0.5334 — chance** |
| **Predictive entropy** | entropy of the merged verdict | **0.7747 — shipped** |
| Entropy + 5× disagreement | both combined | 0.7675 — worse than entropy alone |

**Why disagreement fails:** both heads are trained on the same labels with the
same loss, so they converge toward each other. Their divergence never exceeds
0.316 across the whole test set. The architecture is named for a signal that
does not work, and that is reported rather than dropped.

**Triage bands** are the 60th and 85th percentile of validation entropy — a
property of the seed-0 checkpoint, not a clinical standard.

## 17 — How it compares, and why

**Headline:** Test set 4,006 radiographs · 22.5% positive · AUPRC and AUROC are mean ± SD over three seeds

| Model | Params | AUPRC | AUROC | it/s | Why it lands here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EfficientNet-B0** | 4.01M | **0.6993 ± 0.0033** | **0.8781** | 3.66 | ImageNet features plus compound scaling. Best accuracy at the smallest pretrained size, and the best calibrated (ECE 0.0343). |
| DenseNet121 | 6.96M | 0.6872 ± 0.0034 | 0.8728 | 2.11 | The CheXNet backbone; dense connections reuse features. Slowest of the four — concatenation makes it memory-bandwidth bound, not parameter bound. |
| ResNet50 | 23.51M | 0.6747 ± 0.0072 | 0.8675 | 5.04 | 5.6× EfficientNet's parameters and still third, with the highest seed variance of any model. On 18,677 images more capacity buys instability. |
| **VeritasXR** | **4.22M** | 0.6410 ± 0.0039 | 0.8531 | **7.94** | The only model here with no pretraining — that single difference is worth 5–9% relative AUPRC. Fastest of the five, and it ships a measured uncertainty (entropy, AUROC 0.7747 against its own errors) in the same forward pass: no ensemble, no repeated passes. |
| HOG + linear SVM | 6,085 feat | 0.3823 ± 0.0010 | 0.7283 | — | Hand-crafted gradient histograms cannot represent diffuse opacity texture. The control that shows the deep models are earning their place. |

- **The ordering is significant, not noise:** a paired bootstrap run per seed, Holm-corrected, separates every pair on every seed. The gap to the pretrained models is 0.034–0.058 AUPRC.
- **The cause is pretraining**, not architecture or capacity — and not the shared learning rate either (slide 18).

**Notes:** Lead with this. A panel that discovers the deficit themselves will doubt everything else. The "why" column is what turns a scoreboard into an argument — each row names the mechanism, not just the number.

## 18 — Was the comparison unfair?

**Headline:** The obvious objection, measured — and it points the wrong way

Every deep model retrained at ⅓ and 3× the shared learning rate, seed 0:

| Model | 1e-4 | 3e-4 *(shared)* | 1e-3 | Best − shared |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EfficientNet-B0 | 0.6954 | 0.7009 | **0.7025** | +0.0016 |
| DenseNet121 | **0.6961** | 0.6824 | 0.6668 | **+0.0137** |
| ResNet50 | **0.6880** | 0.6849 | 0.6257 | +0.0031 |
| VeritasXR | 0.6316 | 0.6426 | **0.6445** | +0.0019 |

**One model was genuinely handicapped by the shared rate — DenseNet121, a
baseline.** Tuning every model individually *widens* the gap to VeritasXR. This
is the opposite of the result the probe was run hoping to find.

## 19 — Ablation: what actually earns its place

**Headline:** Seven variants × three seeds. One result survives.

| Variant | Params | AUPRC | Δ vs full | Across seeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `concat_merge` | 4,264,310 | 0.6422 | +0.0011 | sign flips |
| `full` | 4,215,352 | 0.6410 | — | — |
| `fixed_merge` | 4,166,006 | 0.6394 | −0.0016 | sign flips |
| `scalar_merge` | 4,166,008 | 0.6360 | −0.0051 | never separates |
| `no_se` | 4,177,672 | 0.6348 | −0.0062 | sign flips |
| `global_only` | 3,302,108 | 0.6337 | −0.0073 | sign flips |
| **`local_only`** | **1,008,348** | 0.6205 | **−0.0205** | **all 3 seeds** |

- **Only the 7×7 global pathway survives replication.** Removing it costs accuracy on every seed.
- Squeeze-excitation, the merge gate and the merge strategy all **change sign** depending on the seed
- `global_only` separates on seed 0 and then *reverses* on seed 1

## 20 — Four results that reversed on replication

**Headline:** The finding worth carrying out of this project

| Claim from seed 0 | What three seeds showed |
|---|---|
| 12× calibration win for VeritasXR | **Second, not first** (ECE 0.0440 vs EfficientNet's 0.0343); a one-parameter temperature fit puts it **last** |
| The cheap 3×3 branch beats the expensive 7×7 | **Reverses** — seed 0 caught `global_only` at its worst |
| Pathway disagreement measures uncertainty | **AUROC 0.5334 — chance** |
| p < 0.0001 across all paired tests | Weakest is **p = 0.014** once run per seed |

**Every one of these looked like a finding from a single run.** Each was
cleaner, more attractive, and wrong.

**Notes:** This is the slide that distinguishes the project. Deliver it as method, not apology.

## 21 — The input gate: refusing what the model cannot judge

**Image:** `diagrams/5-input-gate-{light|dark}.png`

**Headline:** The classifier cannot abstain, so the refusal happens before it

**The problem, measured on the deployed checkpoint:**
- 4 of 7 ordinary photographs landed in the **highest-confidence** band
- A flat white field was reported as **68% pneumonia**
- A radiograph of a **foot** returned P(pneumonia) = 0.911

**The fix:** a 16×16 layout template fitted on the training split — three cuts
(colour, contrast, distance/correlation against the mean chest), each at the
2nd validation percentile.

| | Turned away |
|---|---|
| Radiographs of the wrong body part (22) | **95%** (21/22) |
| Ordinary photographs (11) | **100%** |
| Rotated / inverted / noise / flat field | 99.8–100% |
| **Genuine chest radiographs — the cost** | **3.8%** |

The one that gets through is a horse hoof. Passing the gate is not evidence an
image *is* a chest radiograph — only that nothing obvious says otherwise.

## 22 — Deployment

**Headline:** 16.9 MB ONNX model, running in the browser

- Exported to ONNX opset 17; `check_onnx_parity.py` asserts ONNX reproduces PyTorch to 2×10⁻⁷
- Runs client-side on WebAssembly — **the image never leaves the machine**
- Five outputs in one pass: verdict, uncertainty, disagreement, two CAMs
- Throughput on an RTX 3070 Ti Laptop: **VeritasXR 7.94 it/s** vs ResNet50 5.04, EfficientNet-B0 3.66, DenseNet121 2.11

**Live:** vanshdev0101.github.io/-VeritasXR-Chest-X-Ray-Diagnosis-with-Clinical-Uncertainty

**Notes:** Speed is the one axis where the custom model leads. Say so plainly — and say it is a consequence of being small and shallow, not of the dual pathway.

## 23 — Limitations

- Single dataset, single task, **no external validation cohort**
- Labels derive from radiologist report review, not confirmed microbiology
- The LR probe is one seed and three rates: it bounds the confound, it does not rule it out
- Uncertainty is predictive entropy — an **aleatoric** signal; the epistemic signal this architecture was designed around does not work
- The input gate is one cheap statistic validated on 22 wrong-body-part images, and one passed
- Triage thresholds belong to the seed-0 checkpoint, not to the architecture

## 24 — Conclusion

**Headline:** The architecture's central hypothesis did not survive measurement — and that is the result

- A 4.22M-parameter CNN trained from scratch reaches **0.6410 AUPRC** against EfficientNet-B0's **0.6993**
- Of its distinctive components, **only the 7×7 global pathway earns its place**
- **The gap is pretraining, not architecture.** Capacity is demonstrably not the limit.
- The lever that would move it: ImageNet initialisation, or self-supervised pretraining on unlabelled chest radiographs — not more blocks

**What the study produces instead:** a measured account of why, and four
documented cases of a single seed reporting a result that reverses on
replication.

**Notes:** Close here. Do not add a "future work will fix it" slide — the honest ending is stronger than an optimistic one.

- **Operating point:** at a fixed 0.5 the F1 ranking contradicts AUPRC; tuned on validation it agrees. VeritasXR is last under either convention.
